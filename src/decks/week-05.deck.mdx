# Factorio: Engineering Addiction

Week 5 — Transport and Defence

---

## Transport options

Walking is the baseline every other option is measured against — each
vehicle buys speed, cargo space, or both, at a different cost.

- the **car** trades a fuel slot for speed well above walking pace, with a
  small cargo hold — cheap, early, and still favoured by some players
  purely for outrunning trouble rather than fighting it
- the **tank** is slower and less maneuverable than a player on foot, but
  carries a submachine gun, a short-range flamethrower and a cannon —
  durability and firepower over mobility
- the **train** (next week's topic) trades route flexibility for speed and
  cargo capacity over long, fixed distances
- the **spidertron** takes no fuel at all — its speed comes only from
  exoskeleton equipment fitted into its own equipment grid, and it can
  cross terrain a wheeled vehicle can't

## Obstacle removal

Cliffs and water aren't obstacles to route around forever — cliff
explosives and landfill remove them permanently, at a deliberate resource
cost.

- cliff explosives clear a cliff face outright, opening a route or building
  site that was previously blocked
- landfill converts water tiles to buildable land, at the cost of the
  landfill material itself
- both are one-way: once placed, the terrain change is permanent, which
  makes it a genuine design decision, not a temporary fix

## Biters, worms and nests

Enemy presence grows along an **evolution factor** from 0 to 1, driven by
three independent things: time passing, cumulative pollution produced, and
nests destroyed. All of it is driven by total pollution *produced*, not by
how much of the cloud you've managed to contain.

- destroying a nest bumps evolution a fixed amount regardless of nest type
  — clearing nests is a safety decision, not a way to make the remaining
  biters weaker
- pollution has a second, separate job: pollution reaching a nest is what
  triggers actual attacks, on its own schedule — containing the cloud
  reduces attack frequency even though it does nothing for evolution
  already accrued
- nests also expand on their own timer, claiming unclaimed territory
  outward, stopping only when they reach an occupied area (another nest,
  or a player structure) — left alone, expansion eventually fills every
  explored area

The curve flattens, it never resets — pollution already produced doesn't
un-produce itself, which is why the practical lever is producing less of
it going forward, not cleaning up what's already out there.

## Turret creep, firepower and kiting

Turret creep advances a defensive line by building the next turret just
inside the range of the one behind it, letting the position push forward
without ever leaving a gap undefended.

- walls only block melee contact — they don't stop projectile or spit
  damage, and big and behemoth biters can reach two tiles, hitting whatever
  is directly behind a single wall layer
- a wall's real job is buying a turret time: it keeps melee attackers at
  arm's length so the turret keeps firing instead of getting hit, not
  making the turret invulnerable
- kiting uses a vehicle's speed advantage (the car, mainly) to stay outside
  an enemy's attack range while still doing damage — useful against
  spitters and worms specifically, since their damage is ranged and
  otherwise ignores your own speed entirely

## Other tools

Not every threat is a straight firepower fight — poison capsules and
combat robots solve problems that turrets and guns don't.

- poison capsules deal heavy damage to worms specifically (little to
  nests), with far more range than a grenade — the direct answer to a worm
  a player can't safely walk up to
- combat robots (defender, distractor and destroyer, in rising cost order)
  follow the player or hold position and fight automatically, useful for
  soaking or distributing damage during an attack run on a nest rather than
  tanking every hit personally
- neither replaces firepower — both exist to change *where* the damage
  lands, buying survivability during an approach a straight gunfight
  wouldn't allow

{/* _class: quote */}

> Walling off a threat isn't clearing it. It's scheduling it for later, with interest.

Evolution keeps accruing on its own timer whether or not you're looking at
it — postponing a nest doesn't shrink the bill.
