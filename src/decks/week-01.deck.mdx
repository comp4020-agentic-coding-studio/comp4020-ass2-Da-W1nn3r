# Factorio: Engineering Addiction

Week 1 — Early-Game Foundations

---

## Before you touch a belt

Read the map first. A few seconds mining a "big rock" outproduces minutes of
drill time. Lay out a coal chain before your first boiler runs dry, not
after.

- prioritise big rocks over patch mining
- coal chains feed boilers, boilers feed engines
- nothing here needs automation yet

---

## Burner chains

Before you have any electricity, everything runs on coal directly. A
**burner chain** is a drill, a furnace, and the burner inserters between
them, each one fed its own fuel — no power grid involved yet.

- burner mining drill → burner inserter → furnace → burner inserter → chest
- every burner inserter needs its own coal, on top of whatever the drill
  and furnace are burning
- slow and fuel-hungry, but it's running the moment you place it — no
  research, no power poles

![Placeholder — a screenshot of a burner mining drill feeding a stone furnace through a burner inserter, with a second burner inserter unloading plates into a chest, will go here](./assets/burner-chain.png)

This is what you're standing on top of for the first few minutes. It's not
meant to last — it's meant to get smelting started before power exists.

---

## Direct burner-to-furnace

A burner mining drill's output faces one direction. Place a furnace right
in front of it and the drill deposits ore straight into the furnace — no
belt, no inserter, no wasted tile.

- saves an inserter and the coal it would burn
- only works for the one furnace directly in front of the drill's output
- still fine to hand-feed both the drill and the furnace their own coal

![Placeholder — a screenshot of a burner mining drill placed directly facing into a stone furnace with no inserter between them will go here](./assets/direct-burner-furnace.png)

Combine this with the furnace-row idea below once you outgrow one drill,
one furnace.

---

## The ore-to-furnace ratio

One mining drill feeds a fixed number of furnaces before the belt runs
empty. Furnace *rows* beat scattered furnaces, because every furnace in a
row is in inserter reach of the same ore and coal belts.

Stone furnace now. Steel furnace once you have the steel to spare — faster
smelt time, same footprint.

---

## Boiler and steam power

Actual electricity starts with three parts in a line: an offshore pump, a
boiler, and a steam engine.

- offshore pump draws water, piped into the boiler
- boiler burns coal to turn that water into steam
- steam engine converts steam into power for the grid
- one boiler comfortably keeps two steam engines fed — build in that pair,
  not one-for-one

![Placeholder — a screenshot of an offshore pump piped into a row of boilers, each boiler piped into a pair of steam engines, all connected to power poles, will go here](./assets/boiler-steam.png)

Get this running before your first burner chain runs out of patience — it's
what everything from Week 2 onward is built on top of.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Automation before almost everything else.

Research priority in one sentence. A couple of early logistics or
efficiency techs are worth taking first — nothing else is.

---

## Hand-fed, on purpose

Your inventory is a temporary buffer between "gathered" and "used," not a
production line.

- craft ammo and a handful of assemblers by hand while the base is small
- everything else waits for a proper line
- knowing which is which is the actual skill this week teaches

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## Design for the base you don't have yet

Leave empty space. Leave belt-lane capacity you don't need today.

This is the one habit that separates a base that scales from one that
doesn't — and it's where Week 2 picks up.

```txt
Goal: research Automation
Using: hand mining, manual smelting, starter power
Not using: belts or assemblers beyond what Automation unlocks
```

Bring the save you finish this tutorial with to Week 2 — base design starts
