# Admin

## Getting Factorio

The course requires a licensed copy of Factorio. It is sold directly by the
developer on [GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/factorio) and on
[Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/427520/Factorio/), without a demo
or free tier — either storefront's full version is fine for this course, and
the two run identically once installed.

## Approved mods

The following mods are approved for use in assessments and tutorials. All of
them are visualisation or convenience tools — none change recipes, ratios,
or game balance, so a save built with them stays comparable to one without:

- [Timelapse Base Edition](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/TLBE) — captures
  screenshots of your base at set intervals for a time-lapse.
- [Disco Science](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/DiscoScience) — labs light
  up in the colour of whatever science pack they're consuming.
- [Production Rates Calculator Extreme](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/sw-rates-calc-extreme) —
  shows production rates for selected buildings.
- [Belt Visualizer](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/belt-visualizer) —
  highlights a belt line and its individual lanes.
- [Circuit Visualizer](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/circuit_visualizer) —
  highlights circuit network connections between combinators.
- [Heat Visualizer](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/heat-visualizer) — overlays
  a temperature scale on heat pipes.
- [Tapeline](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/Tapeline) — measures distances
  and marks out a flat working surface.

Any other mod needs the tutor's sign-off before it touches a submitted save.

## Blueprint provenance

Using a blueprint you found online, in a book, or from a previous
semester's cohort is fine — cite where it came from, the same as any other
source. Presenting someone else's blueprint as your own original design,
in a report or in conversation with a marker, is not. If a submission's
design decisions can't actually be explained by the person who submitted
it, that is treated as a provenance problem, not a knowledge gap.

## Save files and version control

Submissions are the save file named in the assessment brief, at the state
it was in at the deadline. Keep your intermediate saves — a save history
that shows a design arrived at gradually is the easiest way to answer a
question about how a submission was built, and its absence is not treated
as proof of anything on its own.

## Finding your save files

Assignments and tutorials that ask for a save file want the actual `.zip`
save from Factorio's own saves folder, not an exported blueprint or
screenshot. The folder location depends on your OS:

- **Windows:** `%AppData%\Factorio\saves` (i.e.
  `C:\Users\<you>\AppData\Roaming\Factorio\saves`)
- **macOS:** `~/Library/Application Support/factorio/saves`
- **Linux:** `~/.factorio/saves/`

Each save is a single `.zip` file named after the save — submit that file
as-is, without renaming or re-zipping it.

## Group-project conduct

The group project is marked partly on collaboration evidence. Zones and
roles agreed in Week 7 are expected to hold for the rest of the project;
renegotiating them is fine, but silently redoing a teammate's work without
telling them is not, and repeatedly missing agreed contributions is a
matter for the tutor, not something to solve by one person quietly
covering for another.

## Late work and extensions

Extensions are granted on request, before the deadline, for a genuine
reason — ask your tutor rather than submitting late and explaining
afterwards. Weekly practicals are the exception: they are marked as a set
at semester's end, so a single missed week costs less than a missed
assessment deadline does.

## Accessibility and support

If a tutorial's format, a save file, or an assessment's timing doesn't
work for you, tell your tutor or the convenor — the practical work in this
course is flexible in how it's demonstrated, even where the underlying
skill being assessed isn't.

## Factory simulator

The [factory simulator](/simulator/) lays out belts, inserters, assemblers,
and power poles on a grid, runs them, and reports the bottleneck — useful
for testing a balancer, a ratio, or a tileable layout before building it in
Factorio itself. Several tutorials link to it directly; it is also linked
here rather than in the site's main navigation.
